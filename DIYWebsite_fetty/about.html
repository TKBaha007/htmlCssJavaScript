the history

Founded in 1892, Liverpool Football Club is one of the most successful and storied clubs in global football history, boasting 19 League titles and 6 European Cups/Champions Leagues.

Formation and Early Years
1892
Founded by John Houlding after a dispute over the rent at Anfield forced Everton FC to leave for Goodison Park. Left with an empty stadium, Houlding created Liverpool FC, winning their first League title in 1901.

The Bill Shankly Revolution
1959–1974
Bill Shankly took over a struggling Second Division side and transformed Liverpool into a power. He introduced the iconic all-red kit, established the famed "Boot Room" coaching culture, and secured three First Division titles, two FA Cups, and a UEFA Cup.

European Dominance & Bob Paisley Era
1974–1983
Bob Paisley took over from Shankly and established the most successful era in the club's history. Liverpool won 6 League titles and 3 European Cups (1977, 1978, 1981) in just nine seasons.

Success and Tragedy
1985–1989
Joe Fagan guided the team to a treble in 1984, but the 1985 Heysel Stadium disaster led to a six-year ban from European competition. In 1989, the Hillsborough disaster claimed the lives of 97 supporters, fundamentally reshaping English football safety and triggering a decades-long fight for justice.

Miracle of Istanbul
2005
Under Rafael Benítez, Liverpool won their fifth European Cup in historic fashion, coming back from 3–0 down against AC Milan to win on penalties in Istanbul.

The Jürgen Klopp Renaissance
2015–2024
Jürgen Klopp revitalized the club with "heavy metal football," leading Liverpool to victory in the 2019 UEFA Champions League and securing their 19th league title in 2020—their first in 30 years.

The Klopp Era

When Jürgen Klopp arrived at Anfield on October 8, 2015, declaring his mission to transform Liverpool FC from "doubters to believers," he initiated one of the most transformative eras in modern football history.

Over nine years, the charismatic German manager rebuilt the club into an elite global superpower through his high-intensity "heavy metal football" and strategic transfers, bringing in key figures like Mohamed Salah, Sadio Mané, Roberto Firmino, Virgil van Dijk, and Alisson Becker to establish a devastatingly fluid attacking force anchored by world-class defensive stability.

After early heartbreaks in domestic and European finals, Klopp’s reign reached its apex between 2018 and 2020: following a legendary 4–0 comeback against Barcelona in the semi-finals, Liverpool secured their sixth UEFA Champions League title in Madrid in 2019, quickly followed by the FIFA Club World Cup, UEFA Super Cup, and a dominant 2019–20 Premier League title run—accumulating 99 points to end the club's agonizing 30-year top-flight drought.

Even through severe injury crises, Klopp continually adapted, constructing a "Liverpool 2.0" that captured an FA Cup and two Carabao Cups, while relentlessly pursuing unprecedented quadruples and competing in every possible fixture in the 2021–22 season.

By the time he departed in May 2024 after 491 matches in charge, Klopp had won every major trophy available to an English club, leaving behind a complete legacy, an energized academy, and an enduring emotional bond with the Anfield faithful.
